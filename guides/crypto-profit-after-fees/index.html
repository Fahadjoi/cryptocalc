How to calculate crypto profit after fees

The price moved 10% in your favour, yet the balance grew by less. Here is where the difference goes and how to work it out before you trade.

The three numbers in every trade

Every trade has a value when you open it, a value when you close it, and fees at both ends. Profit is the closing value minus the opening value, minus both fees. Leaving out the fees is the most common reason a result on paper does not match the exchange.

Net profit = coins × sell price − amount invested − entry fee − exit feeCoins = amount invested ÷ buy price

A spot trade, step by step

You put $1,000 into Bitcoin at $60,000 and sell at $66,000. The exchange charges 0.1% per trade.

Coins bought: 1,000 ÷ 60,000 = 0.016667 BTC

Value at the sale: 0.016667 × 66,000 = $1,100

Entry fee: 0.1% of $1,000 = $1.00. Exit fee: 0.1% of $1,100 = $1.10

Net profit: 100 − 1.00 − 1.10 = $97.90, a return of 9.79%

The exit fee is charged on what the position is worth when you sell, so a winning trade pays a slightly larger exit fee than a losing one. A calculator that charges both fees on the entry value understates the cost of every profitable trade.

A short trade

A short makes money when the price falls. You sell first and buy back later, so the sums run in the other direction.

You short $1,000 of Bitcoin at $60,000 and buy back at $54,000, at 0.1% per trade.

Coins sold: 0.016667 BTC. Gain per coin: $6,000. Gross profit: $100

Entry fee: $1.00. Exit fee: 0.1% of $900 = $0.90

Net profit: 100 − 1.00 − 0.90 = $98.10

What leverage changes

Leverage multiplies the position, and fees are charged on the position, not on the money you put in. That makes fees a much bigger share of your own money than the percentage on the fee page suggests.

You put in $1,000 at 5×, so the position is $5,000. The price rises 10%.

Gross profit: 10% of $5,000 = $500

Entry fee: 0.1% of $5,000 = $5.00. Exit fee: 0.1% of $5,500 = $5.50

Net profit: $489.50, or 48.95% on your $1,000

The same trade without leverage paid $2.10 in fees. At 5× it pays $10.50. On futures you may also pay or receive funding for every eight hours the position stays open.

Costs that are easy to miss

  • The spread. Instant buy and sell buttons often quote a price slightly worse than the market, on top of the stated fee.
  • Withdrawal and network fees. Moving coins or cash off the exchange is charged separately.
  • Tax. Many countries tax crypto gains. The rules differ widely, so check the ones that apply to you.

Common questions

Is the fee charged on my money or on the whole position?

On the whole position. With 10× leverage and $500 of your own money, a 0.05% fee is charged on $5,000, which is $2.50 each way.

Why does my exchange show a different profit from my own sum?

The usual causes are the exit fee being charged on the exit value, funding payments on futures, and a fill price slightly different from the price you planned for.

Do I pay fees on a losing trade?

Yes. Both fees are charged whether the trade wins or loses, so a loss is always a little larger than the price move alone.

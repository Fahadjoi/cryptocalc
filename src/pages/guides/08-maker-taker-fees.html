Makers add orders, takers use them

An exchange keeps a list of buy and sell orders waiting at different prices, called the order book. An order that waits in the book is a maker order, because it gives other traders something to trade against. An order that is matched straight away with one already waiting is a taker order.

  • Market orders are always taker. They fill at once at the best available price.
  • Limit orders are usually maker. A buy placed below the current price waits in the book until the market comes to it.
  • A limit order can be taker. If you set a buy limit at or above the current selling price, it fills immediately and pays the taker rate.

Exchanges usually charge makers less, because waiting orders are what make a market easy to trade in. Stop-loss orders that trigger as market orders pay the taker rate.

What the difference adds up to

Base rates run from about 0.1% per trade on the largest international exchanges to 0.5% or more on simpler buy-and-sell apps. Small as that looks, it repeats on every order.

Fees on futures

Futures fees look lower, often a few hundredths of a percent, but they are charged on the full leveraged position.

You open a 10× position with $500. The position is $5,000 and the taker fee is 0.05%.

Fee to open: $2.50. Fee to close: about $2.50.

The round trip costs $5, which is 1% of your own money.

Perpetual futures also carry funding payments, which are separate from trading fees.

Costs that are not on the fee page

  • Spread on instant buys. One-tap buy features often build their charge into the price you are quoted. The fee may read as zero while the price is 0.5% to 1% worse than the market.
  • Withdrawal fees. Moving coins out costs a fixed amount per withdrawal that depends on the coin and the network.
  • Deposit and card fees. Buying with a card usually costs far more than a bank transfer.

Ways to pay less

  • Use limit orders when you do not need to be filled this second.
  • Use the exchange's trading screen, not the instant-buy button, for anything beyond small amounts.
  • Check the fee tiers. Most exchanges lower rates as your 30-day volume grows, and some give a discount for paying fees in their own token.

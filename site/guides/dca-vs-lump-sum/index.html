Dollar-cost averaging vs lump sum

Buying a fixed amount on a schedule takes the timing decision away. Whether it also earns more than buying all at once depends on what the price did next.

How DCA works

With dollar-cost averaging you invest the same amount of money at regular intervals, whatever the price is. The fixed amount buys more coins when the price is low and fewer when it is high, so your average cost ends up below the average of the prices you bought at.

When DCA comes out ahead

You buy $100 of Bitcoin four times, at $50,000, $40,000, $25,000 and $40,000.

Coins: 0.002 + 0.0025 + 0.004 + 0.0025 = 0.011 BTC

Average cost: 400 ÷ 0.011 = $36,364, against an average price of $38,750

At $40,000 the holding is worth $440, a gain of 10%.

Putting the whole $400 in at the first price of $50,000 would have bought 0.008 BTC, worth $320 at the end. The price fell after the first buy, so spreading the buys helped.

When a lump sum comes out ahead

The same four $100 buys, at $25,000, $40,000, $50,000 and $62,500.

Coins: 0.004 + 0.0025 + 0.002 + 0.0016 = 0.0101 BTC

At $62,500 the holding is worth $631, a gain of 58%.

A lump sum of $400 at the first price of $25,000 would have bought 0.016 BTC, worth $1,000. When the price rises steadily, every later buy is more expensive than the first, and waiting costs money.

So which is better?

Neither wins every time. A lump sum does better when the price mostly rises afterwards, and DCA does better when it falls or swings first. Nobody knows in advance which is coming. What DCA reliably offers is something else:

  • It suits how most people earn. Money arrives monthly, so regular buying is often the only option.
  • It removes the worst case. You cannot put everything in at the top.
  • It is easier to keep doing. A plan that does not depend on judging the market is less likely to be abandoned after a fall.

Watch the fees on small buys

Percentage fees cost the same whether you buy once or fifty times. Fixed and minimum fees do not. A $1 minimum fee on a $10 daily buy is 10% of every purchase, and the same $1 on a $300 monthly buy is 0.33%. If your exchange has a minimum fee, fewer and larger buys keep more of your money.

Test it on real prices

The DCA backtest replays up to five years of actual daily prices for any amount and schedule, and compares the result with a single buy on the first day. Past prices show what would have happened. They do not show what will.

Common questions

How often should I buy?

Weekly and monthly give similar results over long periods. Choose the schedule that matches your income and keeps fees low.

Does DCA guarantee a profit?

No. If the price ends below your average cost, you have a loss. DCA lowers the average cost in a falling market but cannot make the coin go up.

Is DCA only for Bitcoin?

It works for any asset. It is safest with assets you expect to exist for years, since the method depends on holding through falls.

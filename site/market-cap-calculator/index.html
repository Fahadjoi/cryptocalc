How market cap and price fit together

A coin's market cap is its price multiplied by the number of coins in circulation. Turn that around and you get the price any market cap implies. That is the whole idea behind this calculator, and it is the fastest way to test a price prediction before you believe it.

Price = market cap ÷ circulating supplyMarket cap = price × circulating supply. Both use the same currency.

A worked example

A coin trades at $0.50 with 40 billion coins in circulation, so its market cap is 0.50 × 40,000,000,000 = $20 billion.

Someone says it will reach $10. At $10 the market cap would be 10 × 40,000,000,000 = $400 billion, twenty times today.

Set the target market cap to $400 billion and the calculator shows the same $10 price and the 20× multiple, so you can compare that with the coins that are worth $400 billion today.

Why a low price does not mean a coin is cheap

A coin at $0.01 can be more expensive than one at $60,000. What matters is how many coins exist. A token with 500 trillion coins in circulation needs a market cap of $5 trillion to reach a single cent more than $0.01 per coin. The price per coin on its own tells you nothing about room to grow. The market cap does.

Comparing with other coins

The table under the result shows the price your coin would have if it were worth as much as each of the large coins today. It is a quick reality check. If a target needs a market cap bigger than Ethereum's, the coin has to become one of the largest assets in crypto to get there.

Circulating, total and maximum supply

  • Circulating supply is what is trading now. The live market cap uses this figure.
  • Total supply adds coins that exist but are locked, for example team and investor allocations.
  • Maximum supply is the most that can ever exist. Bitcoin's is 21 million. Some coins have none.

When locked coins are released, the supply goes up. For the price to stay the same, the market cap has to grow by the same share. Enter the maximum supply in the supply field to see the price a target implies once everything is unlocked, which market watchers call the fully diluted value.

What this calculator does not tell you

A market cap is not money that has gone into a coin, and a price target is not a forecast. The calculator only shows what a target would mean in numbers. Whether enough buyers will turn up to get there is a separate question. For a longer look at the maths, read market cap maths.

Common questions

How do I calculate the price of a coin at a given market cap?

Divide the market cap by the circulating supply. A coin with 1 billion coins in circulation would be priced at $100 if its market cap reached $100 billion.

Can a coin with a huge supply reach $1?

Only if the market cap reaches the supply in dollars. A coin with 500 billion coins in circulation needs a $500 billion market cap to be worth $1, which is larger than almost every coin today.

What is fully diluted valuation?

It is the price multiplied by the maximum supply, the market cap the coin would have if every coin that will ever exist were already in circulation. Enter the maximum supply in the supply field to see prices on that basis.

Where does the live supply come from?

The calculator takes the live market cap and price from CoinGecko and divides one by the other. That gives the circulating supply CoinGecko uses, which can differ slightly from other sites.

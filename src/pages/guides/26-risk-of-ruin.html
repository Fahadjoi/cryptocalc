Losing streaks are normal

Even a strategy that wins half the time will string several losses together, purely by chance. Over 100 trades at a 50% win rate:

  • a run of 5 losses in a row happens about 81% of the time,
  • a run of 8 about 17% of the time,
  • a run of 10 about 4% of the time.

Strategies that win less often, such as trend following with large targets, see much longer runs. At a 40% win rate, a run of 8 losses shows up in about half of all 100-trade stretches. Over 500 trades at 50%, a run of 8 happens more often than not. Plan for these runs before they happen, because they will.

What a streak costs at different risk levels

If you risk a fixed share of the account on each trade, every loss is taken from a slightly smaller balance. After a run of losses the account is down by:

At 1% per trade, ten straight losses cost under 10%, which needs about an 11% gain to recover. At 10% per trade the same run costs 65%, which needs a gain of nearly 190%. The loss recovery calculator shows the gain needed for any drawdown.

What risk of ruin means

Risk of ruin is the chance that losses take the account below the point where you can keep trading, or below a limit you set for yourself, such as a 50% drawdown. It rises sharply with the amount risked per trade and falls as your expectancy improves. A strategy with a small edge and large positions can have a high risk of ruin even though it makes money on average, because a bad run arrives before the edge has time to show.

Keeping it low

  • Risk a small, fixed share per trade. Most trading books settle on 0.5% to 2%. The position size calculator turns that into a position size from your stop.
  • Make sure the strategy has an edge. Without positive expectancy, small risk only slows the decline. See win rate and expectancy.
  • Set a drawdown limit. Many traders halve their risk after a set drawdown, such as 10%, and stop to review after 20%.
  • Watch leverage. Leverage does not change the risk you planned if you size from the stop, but a stop beyond the liquidation price means the loss is the whole margin instead.

Why the feeling matters

Long losing runs are hard to sit through. Traders often abandon a working strategy in the middle of a normal streak, or double their size to win it back, which is how most accounts are actually lost. Knowing in advance that a run of eight or ten losses is ordinary makes it easier to keep following the plan.

Maker and taker fees

Exchanges charge two different rates depending on how your order meets the market. A taker order fills immediately against orders already waiting, which is what a market order does. A maker order is a limit order that sits in the order book until someone else trades against it. Makers add liquidity, so exchanges usually charge them less.

On Binance, Bybit, KuCoin, Bitget and Gate the two rates are the same at the entry level, so order type makes no difference to the fee. On OKX, MEXC, Kraken Pro and Coinbase Advanced a limit order is cheaper, and on Kraken Pro it costs half as much.

A worked example

You buy $1,000 of Bitcoin with a market order and later sell it the same way. That is two taker orders.

At 0.10% each order costs $1.00, so the round trip costs $2.00.

At 0.80% each order costs $8.00, so the round trip costs $16.00. The trade has to gain 1.6% before it makes anything.

Spot fees and futures fees

Futures rates look far lower than spot rates, typically 0.02% for makers and 0.05% for takers against 0.10% on spot. The catch is what they are charged on. A futures fee applies to the whole leveraged position, so a $1,000 deposit at 10× pays 0.05% of $10,000, which is $5, the same as 0.5% of your own money. Enter the full position size, not your margin, when you compare futures.

Futures also carry funding payments every eight hours, which this table does not show. The profit calculator includes them.

Why the table uses base-tier rates

Every exchange lowers its fees as your 30-day volume grows, but the starting tier is what a new or occasional trader pays, and the tiers are where exchanges differ most. Kraken Pro starts at 0.40% maker and 0.80% taker and drops to 0.30% and 0.60% once you pass $2,500 in 30-day volume. Coinbase Advanced lists 0.50% maker and 0.90% taker as its United States entry rate, 0.25% and 0.50% in the EU and UK, and 0.09% and 0.10% in several other markets. The big offshore exchanges sit close to 0.10% from the first trade.

Four ways to pay less

  • Use limit orders where maker rates are lower. You give up the guarantee of an instant fill in return.
  • Pay fees in the exchange token. Binance takes 25% off spot fees paid in BNB and KuCoin takes 20% off when paid in KCS. You have to hold a token whose own price moves.
  • Trade less often. Fees scale with the number of orders. Twenty $1,000 orders a month at 0.10% cost $240 a year, and at 0.80% they cost $1,920.
  • Check your tier. If you are close to the next volume tier, the lower rate applies to every order after you cross it.

Costs the fee table does not show

The trading fee is only the visible part of the cost. A low fee on a thin market can still be the expensive choice.

  • Spread: the gap between the best buy and sell price. On small coins and quiet exchanges it can be larger than the fee.
  • Slippage: large market orders eat through several price levels and fill at a worse average.
  • Withdrawal fees: a fixed amount per withdrawal that varies by coin and network.
  • Instant-buy markups: the simple "buy" buttons in exchange apps often charge far more than the trading screen of the same company.

Common questions

Which crypto exchange has the lowest trading fees?

At base-tier spot rates, MEXC lists the lowest with 0% for limit orders and 0.05% for market orders, followed by OKX at 0.08% and 0.10%. Binance, Bybit, KuCoin, Bitget and Gate all charge 0.10% for both.

What is the difference between a maker fee and a taker fee?

You pay the taker fee when your order fills immediately, as a market order does. You pay the maker fee when your limit order waits in the order book and someone else fills it.

Are fees charged when I buy and when I sell?

Yes. Each order is charged separately, so buying and later selling costs two fees. Set orders per month to count both sides.

Why are Coinbase and Kraken more expensive?

Their entry tiers are priced higher than the large offshore exchanges, and both lower the rate as your 30-day volume grows. Many traders accept the higher cost for local regulation and bank transfers in their own currency.

Do these rates apply to futures?

Switch the market to Futures to see perpetual futures rates. They are commonly about 0.02% maker and 0.05% taker, and they are charged on the full leveraged position, not on your margin.

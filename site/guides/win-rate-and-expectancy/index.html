Win rate and expectancy

A high win rate does not make a strategy profitable, and a low one does not make it a loser. What matters is how much you make on the winners compared with what you lose on the losers.

Win rate on its own tells you little

Win rate is the share of trades that end in profit. A trader who wins 80% of the time sounds skilled, but if each win makes $20 and each loss costs $100, ten trades make 8 × 20 − 2 × 100 = −$40. The strategy loses money while almost always winning.

To judge a strategy you need three numbers together: how often it wins, the average size of a win and the average size of a loss.

The expectancy formula

Expectancy is the average amount you can expect to make or lose per trade over a large number of trades.

Expectancy = (win rate × average win) − (loss rate × average loss)Loss rate = 1 − win rate. Use averages after fees.

You win 40% of trades. The average win is $150 and the average loss is $50.

Expectancy: 0.40 × 150 − 0.60 × 50 = 60 − 30 = +$30 per trade.

Over 100 trades that is about $3,000, even though six trades in ten lose.

Thinking in R

Many traders measure results in R, where 1R is the amount risked on a trade. If you always risk $50, a $150 win is +3R and a full loss is −1R. Expectancy in R does not change when your account grows, so it is easier to compare over time.

Same strategy: 0.40 × 3R − 0.60 × 1R = +0.6R per trade.

Risking 1% of the account each time, 100 trades add roughly 60% before compounding.

Break-even win rate

For a given reward-to-risk ratio there is a minimum win rate below which a strategy loses money.

Break-even win rate = 1 ÷ (1 + reward ÷ risk)
  • Reward 1, risk 1: you need to win 50% of the time.
  • Reward 2, risk 1: 33.3%.
  • Reward 3, risk 1: 25%.

The position size calculator shows this number for each trade once you enter a take-profit, and the risk-reward ratio covers it in more detail.

Fees move the line

Every trade pays fees whether it wins or not. They make wins a little smaller and losses a little larger, so the real break-even win rate is always slightly higher than the formula gives. Strategies that aim for small moves, such as scalping, feel this most. A 0.2% round trip in fees takes a large share of a 0.5% target.

How many trades you need

Ten trades say almost nothing. A strategy that wins 40% of the time can easily lose seven or eight in a row by chance. Most traders want at least 50 to 100 trades, kept in a simple log with entry, stop, exit and fees, before trusting the numbers. Keep the rules the same throughout, or you are measuring several strategies mixed together.

Expectancy is not the whole story

Two strategies with the same expectancy can feel very different. One with a low win rate brings long losing streaks, which are hard to sit through and can push you to break your own rules. Read losing streaks and risk of ruin to see how big those streaks get.

Common questions

What is a good win rate for trading?

There is no single good number. A 35% win rate is profitable if wins are well over twice the size of losses, and an 80% win rate can lose money if losses are much bigger than wins.

How do I calculate trading expectancy?

Multiply your win rate by your average win, multiply your loss rate by your average loss, and subtract the second from the first. A positive result means the strategy made money on average.

Should I include fees in my averages?

Yes. Use the profit or loss each trade actually added to your balance, after fees and funding, or the result will look better than it is.

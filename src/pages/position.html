What position sizing does

Most traders pick a size first and find out what they lost afterwards. Position sizing reverses that. You choose the loss you can accept, and the size follows from how far away your stop is. A wide stop gives a small position, a tight stop gives a larger one, and the money at risk stays the same either way.

Coins to buy = (balance × risk %) ÷ (|entry − stop| + fees per coin)Position size = coins × entry price. Fees per coin = entry × fee + stop × fee.

A worked example

Your account holds $10,000 and you risk 1%, so the most you will lose is $100.

You plan to buy at $60,000 with a stop at $58,200. The stop is $1,800 away, which is 3% of the entry.

Ignoring fees, coins: 100 ÷ 1,800 = 0.05556 BTC. Position size: 0.05556 × 60,000 = $3,333.

With a 0.1% fee each way, a stopped trade also pays about $118 per coin in fees, so the size drops to 100 ÷ 1,918 = 0.05213 BTC, or $3,128. Either way, a hit stop costs $100.

Choosing a risk percentage

Risking 1% per trade means ten losses in a row cost a little under 10% of the account. At 5% the same streak costs about 40%, and winning that back needs a 67% gain. Small risk keeps a losing run survivable, which is why 1% to 2% is the range most trading books settle on.

  • 0.5% to 1%: a bad month is an inconvenience. Suits new traders and anyone trading often.
  • 2%: the usual upper limit. Five straight losses cost about 10%.
  • Above 3%: drawdowns get deep quickly and it becomes hard to trade calmly.

Leverage does not change your risk, but it can break your stop

With a fixed stop and a fixed risk amount, leverage only changes how much margin the position ties up. A $3,333 position needs $3,333 on spot and $667 at 5×. The loss at the stop is $100 in both cases.

The danger is liquidation. At high leverage the exchange closes your position before the price reaches your stop, and you lose the whole margin. As a rule of thumb, leverage must stay below 100 divided by your stop distance in percent. With a 3% stop, anything near 33× is liquidated first. The calculator checks this for you and warns when the stop will not protect you.

Risk to reward and win rate

If you add a take-profit, the calculator compares the distance to your target with the distance to your stop. A ratio of 1 to 2 means you make $2 for every $1 risked.

Break-even win rate = 1 ÷ (1 + reward ÷ risk)

At 1 to 2 you only need to win 33.3% of trades to break even before fees. At 1 to 1 you need 50%, and at 1 to 0.5 you need 66.7%. A strategy that wins often can live with a low ratio, and one that wins rarely needs a high one.

Where the stop should go

Pick the stop from the chart first and let the size adjust, never the other way round. A stop belongs at the price where your reason for the trade is wrong: under the low that defined the setup for a long, above the high for a short. Moving a stop closer just to afford a bigger position is the most common way to turn a good plan into a quick loss.

What this calculator leaves out

  • Funding: perpetual futures charge or pay funding every eight hours, which the profit calculator can include.
  • Slippage: in a fast market a stop can fill below its price.
  • Your exact maintenance margin: the liquidation check assumes 0.5%. Set your own in the liquidation calculator.
@faq Q: How do I calculate position size in crypto? A: Multiply your account balance by the percentage you are willing to risk, then divide by the distance between your entry and stop-loss prices. That gives the number of coins. Multiply by the entry price for the position size in dollars. Q: What is the 1% rule? A: It means never risking more than 1% of your account on a single trade. With $10,000 that is $100 per trade. It refers to the loss if your stop is hit, not to the size of the position, which can be much larger. Q: Does higher leverage mean a bigger position? A: Not when you size by risk. The position size comes from your risk amount and stop distance. Leverage only lowers the margin you need to hold it, and brings the liquidation price closer. Q: What is a good risk to reward ratio? A: Many traders look for at least 1 to 2, which breaks even with a 33.3% win rate. The right ratio depends on how often your setups win, so track your own results before fixing a rule. Q: Why does the calculator say my position was sized down? A: A very tight stop can call for a position larger than your balance and leverage can open. In that case the calculator shows the largest position you can afford and the smaller amount you would really be risking. @end

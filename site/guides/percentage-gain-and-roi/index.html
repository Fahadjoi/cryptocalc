Percentage gain, ROI and annual return

"Up 200%" can mean very different things depending on fees, time and where you started counting. Three short formulas cover almost every case.

Percentage change

The basic question is how far a price or a balance has moved compared with where it started.

Percentage change = (new value − old value) ÷ old value × 100

You bought a coin at $2.00 and it now trades at $2.60.

(2.60 − 2.00) ÷ 2.00 × 100 = +30%.

If it fell to $1.40 instead: (1.40 − 2.00) ÷ 2.00 × 100 = −30%.

Always divide by the starting value. Dividing by the new value is the most common slip, and it makes gains look smaller and losses look larger than they really are.

Return on investment, after fees

A price move is not the same as what you made. Return on investment, or ROI, compares the money you got back with the money you put in, after every cost.

ROI = (money out − money in) ÷ money in × 100Money in includes the buying fee. Money out is what reaches your balance after the selling fee.

You spend $1,000 plus a $1 fee, so money in is $1,001.

The coin rises 30% and the position is worth $1,300. Selling costs a 0.1% fee of $1.30, so money out is $1,298.70.

ROI: (1,298.70 − 1,001) ÷ 1,001 × 100 = 29.74%, a little under the 30% price move.

On one trade the difference is small. Across hundreds of trades it adds up, which is why the profit calculator counts the fee at both ends.

Annualised return

A 50% gain in three months and a 50% gain in three years are not equally good. To compare returns over different lengths of time, convert them to a yearly rate. This is called the compound annual growth rate, or CAGR.

Annual return = (end value ÷ start value)1 ÷ years − 1

$1,000 grew to $3,000 over 3 years.

(3,000 ÷ 1,000)1/3 − 1 = 30.333 − 1 = 44.2% a year.

Not 66.7% a year, which is what you get by simply dividing the 200% total by three. Compounding means each year's gain builds on the last.

Gains and losses do not cancel out

A coin that rises 50% and then falls 50% does not end where it started. $100 becomes $150, then $75, a 25% loss overall. Percentages are always measured from the current value, so a loss after a gain hits a bigger number. The loss recovery calculator shows how steep the climb back is after a fall.

Percent and percentage points

If a staking rate goes from 4% to 6%, it has risen by 2 percentage points, or by 50%. Both are correct, but they describe different things, and headlines often pick whichever sounds bigger.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving out fees and funding. On leveraged trades, fees are charged on the full position and funding is paid every eight hours.
  • Ignoring deposits. If you added money during the period, the balance grew partly because of your deposits. Measure the return on each deposit separately, or use the money-in and money-out totals.
  • Mixing currencies. A 20% gain in dollars can be a different number in rupees or euros if the exchange rate moved over the same period.

Common questions

How do I calculate my percentage gain on a crypto trade?

Subtract what you paid from what it is worth now, divide by what you paid, and multiply by 100. Bought at $50 and now worth $65: (65 − 50) ÷ 50 × 100 = 30%.

What is the difference between ROI and percentage change?

Percentage change measures the price move. ROI measures what you actually made on the money you put in, after fees and any other costs.

How do I compare returns over different time periods?

Convert each to an annual rate with the CAGR formula: (end ÷ start) raised to the power of 1 ÷ years, minus 1.

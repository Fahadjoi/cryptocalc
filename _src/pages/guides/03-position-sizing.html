Start from the loss, not the size

Position sizing answers one question: if this trade hits its stop-loss, how much do I lose? You choose that amount first, as a percentage of your account. The size of the position then follows from how far away the stop is.

Coins to buy = (account balance × risk %) ÷ (entry price − stop price)Position size = coins × entry price

A worked example

Your account holds $10,000 and you risk 1% per trade, which is $100.

You plan to buy Bitcoin at $60,000 with a stop at $58,200, which is $1,800 or 3% below.

Coins: 100 ÷ 1,800 = 0.05556 BTC

Position size: 0.05556 × 60,000 = $3,333

Check it: a 3% fall on a $3,333 position loses $100. A third of the account is in the trade, but only 1% of it is at risk.

The stop decides the size

Keep the same $100 of risk and move the stop, and the position changes a lot.

Stop distancePosition for a $100 lossShare of a $10,000 account
5%$2,00020%
3%$3,33333%
1%$10,000100%
0.5%$20,000200%, needs 2× leverage

This is the sensible use of leverage: it is what a tight stop requires, and the loss at the stop is still $100. Choosing 20× first and working out the stop afterwards gets it backwards. Before using leverage, check that your liquidation price sits beyond your stop, or the exchange will close the trade before your stop does.

Why 1% or 2% and not 10%

Losing streaks happen to every strategy. The table shows what ten losses in a row do to an account at different risk levels.

Risk per tradeAccount after 10 lossesGain needed to recover
1%down 9.6%10.6%
2%down 18.3%22.4%
5%down 40.1%67.0%
10%down 65.1%186.8%

Losses compound against you. A 50% loss needs a 100% gain to get back to where you started, which is why small, fixed risk matters more than any single entry.

Remember the fees and the gap

  • Fees add to the loss. On the $3,333 example at 0.1% per trade, the two fees come to about $6.57, so the real loss at the stop is nearer $107.
  • Stops can slip. In a fast market a stop order may fill below its price. Leave room for that when you choose the risk percentage.
  • Count open trades together. Three positions in coins that move together are closer to one large position than to three small ones.
@faq Q: What percentage should I risk per trade? A: Many traders use between 0.5% and 2% of the account. There is no correct figure, but the losing-streak table shows how quickly higher numbers become hard to recover from. Q: Does position sizing work without a stop-loss? A: Not really. Without a price at which you exit, the amount at risk is the whole position, and the formula has nothing to work with. Q: Should I size from my whole balance or only what is on the exchange? A: Use the money you have set aside for trading. Sizing from savings you never intend to trade overstates what you can afford to lose. @end

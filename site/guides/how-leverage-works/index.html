How leverage works in crypto trading

Leverage does not change how much the price moves. It changes how much of your own money each move is worth, in both directions.

Margin and position size

The money you put up is the margin. Leverage is how many times larger the position is than the margin.

Position size = margin × leverage

You put up $500 of margin at 10x.

Position size: 500 × 10 = $5,000 of the coin.

Profit and loss are multiplied

The position gains or loses exactly what $5,000 of the coin would. Measured against your $500, that is ten times the price move.

Return on margin ≈ price move × leverage

Price up 3%: the position makes $150, which is +30% on your $500.

Price down 3%: the position loses $150, which is −30% on your $500.

How far the price can move before liquidation

The exchange closes the position before your losses exceed your margin. Ignoring fees and the maintenance margin the exchange keeps back, the margin is gone after a move of about 1 ÷ leverage.

LeveragePosition from $500Move that wipes out the margin
2x$1,00050%
5x$2,50020%
10x$5,00010%
20x$10,0005%
50x$25,0002%
100x$50,0001%

The real liquidation price is a little closer than the table shows, because the exchange liquidates while some margin is still left to cover the maintenance requirement. The liquidation calculator includes it, and liquidation price explained walks through the formula.

Fees and funding are charged on the whole position

Trading fees are a percentage of the position, not of your margin, so leverage multiplies them too.

A 0.05% taker fee on a $5,000 position is $2.50 to open and about $2.50 to close.

That $5 round trip is 1% of your $500 margin before the price has moved at all.

On perpetual futures, funding is also paid or received on the full position size every funding period. See funding rates explained.

Using leverage with a plan

  • Decide your stop first. Pick the price where the trade idea is wrong, then make sure liquidation sits well beyond it.
  • Size by risk, not by leverage. A 2% stop on a $5,000 position risks $100 whether you open it at 5x or 20x. Higher leverage only means less margin is tied up and liquidation is closer. See position sizing.
  • Remember the recovery maths. Leverage turns small price moves into large losses of margin, and large losses are slow to win back. See recovering from a loss.

Common questions

Does 10x leverage mean I can lose ten times my money?

On most crypto exchanges with isolated margin, no. You can lose the margin you put into that position, and it gets lost ten times faster than without leverage.

Is higher leverage riskier if the position size is the same?

The profit and loss in dollars are the same. With higher leverage you put up less margin, so the liquidation price is closer and a smaller move closes the trade.

Why was I liquidated before the price moved 10% at 10x?

Exchanges liquidate while a maintenance margin is still left, and fees and funding reduce your margin along the way. Both bring the liquidation price closer than the simple 1 ÷ leverage figure.

Guides

The sums behind a trade, explained with worked examples you can check by hand. Each guide links to the calculator that does the same maths with live prices.

  • How to calculate crypto profit after feesThe price moved 10% in your favour, yet the balance grew by less. Here is where the difference goes and how to work it out before you trade.
  • Break-even price: how far a coin must move to cover feesSelling at the price you bought at is a loss, because you paid a fee going in and pay another coming out. The break-even price is the first price at which you get all your money back.
  • Position sizing: how much to buy on each tradeMost traders pick a size first and a stop second. Doing it the other way round is what keeps one bad trade from deciding the month.
  • Risk-reward ratio and the win rate you needA trader who is right 35% of the time can make money, and one who is right 70% of the time can lose it. The ratio between the average win and the average loss decides which.
  • How liquidation price works on leveraged tradesWith leverage, the exchange lends you most of the position. Liquidation is the price at which your own share is nearly gone and the exchange closes the trade to protect its loan.
  • Isolated vs cross marginThe margin mode is one switch on the order form, and it decides whether a bad trade can lose only its own stake or the whole futures balance.
  • Average cost and averaging downAfter a few buys at different prices, the number that matters is the one price at which the whole holding breaks even. It is rarely the middle of the prices you paid.
  • Maker and taker fees explainedTwo people can place the same size order on the same exchange and pay different fees. The difference comes from how the order reaches the market.
  • Funding rates on perpetual futuresA perpetual future never expires, so something has to keep its price close to the real coin. That something is funding, a payment that passes between longs and shorts several times a day.
  • Dollar-cost averaging vs lump sumBuying a fixed amount on a schedule takes the timing decision away. Whether it also earns more than buying all at once depends on what the price did next.
  • APR vs APY in stakingTwo platforms can advertise 10% and pay different amounts. One is quoting the rate before compounding and the other after it.
  • Why you get less than the converter showsA converter shows the mid-market rate, the midpoint between what buyers bid and sellers ask. Nobody trades at exactly that price, and four separate costs sit between it and the money that reaches you.

The formula

Break-even sell price = buy price × (1 + fee) ÷ (1 − fee)Fee is the rate per trade written as a decimal, so 0.1% is 0.001

You buy Bitcoin at $60,000 on an exchange that charges 0.1% per trade.

Break-even: 60,000 × 1.001 ÷ 0.999 = $60,120.12

The price has to rise about 0.2% before the trade is worth closing.

The move is a little more than twice the fee. For a quick estimate, doubling the fee rate is close enough.

Why it matters more for short-term trades

A long-term holder aiming for a 50% gain barely notices a 0.2% hurdle. A trader aiming for 1% per trade on a 0.5% fee exchange has to be right by more than 1% just to stand still. The shorter your target, the more the fee rate decides whether a strategy can work at all.

Break-even with leverage

Leverage does not change the price move needed to break even. It changes what the fees cost you as a share of your own money.

You open a 10× futures position with $500, so the position is $5,000. The taker fee is 0.05%.

Fees: $2.50 to open and about $2.50 to close, $5.00 in total

That is 0.1% of the position but 1% of your $500.

Ten trades like this cost a tenth of your margin in fees before any of them wins or loses.

Other things that push break-even further away

  • Spread. On thinly traded coins the gap between the buy and sell price can be bigger than the fee.
  • Funding. On perpetual futures, funding payments add a cost for each period you hold.
  • Averaging. If you bought in several parts, your break-even starts from your average cost, not your latest buy.
@faq Q: Is break-even the same as my buy price? A: No. It is your buy price plus enough to cover the fee you paid to buy and the fee you will pay to sell. Q: How do I find break-even for a short? A: Turn the formula around: break-even buy-back price = sell price × (1 − fee) ÷ (1 + fee). The price has to fall by about twice the fee rate. Q: Does a lower fee tier change my break-even? A: Yes, directly. Halving the fee rate roughly halves the move you need, which is why frequent traders pay attention to fee tiers. @end

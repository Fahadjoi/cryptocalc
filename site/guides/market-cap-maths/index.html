Market cap maths: what price a coin needs for a given market cap

"Can this coin reach $100?" has an answer you can work out in one line. The answer depends on how many coins exist, not just on the price.

The one formula

Market cap = price × circulating supply

Circulating supply is the number of coins that exist and can be traded today. Rearrange the formula and you get the price a coin would need for any market cap you have in mind.

Price needed = target market cap ÷ circulating supply

Checking a price target

A coin trades at $2 with 500 million coins in circulation.

Market cap today: 2 × 500,000,000 = $1 billion.

For the coin to reach $100, its market cap would need to be 100 × 500,000,000 = $50 billion, fifty times its current size.

The second number is the useful one. A price of $100 sounds similar for every coin, but a coin with ten times the supply needs a market cap ten times larger to get there. Comparing the market cap a target implies with the largest coins in the market is a quick way to tell an ambitious target from an impossible one.

Circulating supply versus fully diluted value

Many coins have more tokens to come: team and investor allocations that unlock over time, or rewards paid out to stakers and miners.

Fully diluted value = price × maximum supplyWhere there is no maximum supply, the total supply that exists or is scheduled is used instead.

The same coin has a maximum supply of 2 billion.

Fully diluted value: 2 × 2,000,000,000 = $4 billion, four times its market cap.

If circulating supply grows to 1 billion while the market cap stays at $1 billion, the price falls to $1.

A large gap between market cap and fully diluted value means new supply will keep arriving. Demand has to grow just to hold the price steady.

What market cap does not tell you

  • It is not money invested. Market cap multiplies the last trade price by every coin in circulation. Far less money actually went in, and far less could come out if everyone sold.
  • Thin coins are easy to move. A coin with little trading volume can show a large market cap from a small amount of buying.
  • Supply figures differ between sites. Data providers sometimes count circulating supply differently, especially for locked or burned tokens, so the same coin can show different market caps.

Common questions

How do I work out what price a coin needs to reach a market cap?

Divide the target market cap by the coin's circulating supply. A $10 billion target for a coin with 200 million coins in circulation means a price of $50.

Which is more useful, market cap or fully diluted value?

Market cap shows the coin's size today. Fully diluted value shows its size once all planned tokens exist. Looking at both shows how much new supply is still to come.

Why can't a low-priced coin easily reach $1?

If it has hundreds of billions of coins in circulation, a $1 price would mean a market cap of hundreds of billions of dollars. The supply decides how realistic a price is.

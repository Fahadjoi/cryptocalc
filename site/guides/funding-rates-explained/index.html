Funding rates on perpetual futures

A perpetual future never expires, so something has to keep its price close to the real coin. That something is funding, a payment that passes between longs and shorts several times a day.

Who pays whom

Funding is paid between traders. The exchange passes it along and does not keep it.

  • Positive rate: longs pay shorts. This happens when the future trades above the coin's spot price, usually because more traders want to be long.
  • Negative rate: shorts pay longs. This happens when the future trades below spot.

Most exchanges settle funding every eight hours. Some use four hours or one hour, especially on volatile markets. You pay or receive only if you hold a position at the moment of settlement.

Working out the payment

Funding payment = position value × funding ratePosition value is the full leveraged size, not your margin

You hold a $10,000 long and the rate is 0.01% per eight hours.

Each payment: 10,000 × 0.0001 = $1

Three payments a day: $3. Over 30 days: $90

Kept up for a year, 0.01% every eight hours comes to about 10.95% of the position.

Why leverage makes it bite

Funding is charged on the position but paid out of your margin. The higher the leverage, the bigger the share of your own money each payment takes.

Rates also rise in strong markets. At 0.05% per eight hours, the same $10,000 long pays $15 a day. Because the payments come out of margin, they slowly move the liquidation price closer.

What to do with this

  • Check the rate before a trade you plan to hold. For a trade lasting an hour it hardly matters. For one lasting weeks it can exceed the trading fees many times over.
  • Check which side is paying. When the rate is negative, a long position is paid to stay open.
  • Do not treat the current rate as fixed. It resets each period and can change sign.
  • Include it in the sum. The profit calculator has a funding rate and days-held field for this.

Common questions

Do I pay funding on spot trades?

No. Funding exists only on perpetual futures. Buying and holding the coin itself has no funding cost.

If I close before the funding time, do I pay?

No. Funding is charged only on positions open at the settlement moment.

Can funding make a profitable trade lose money?

Yes. A long held for weeks at a high positive rate can pay more in funding than the price move earns, especially at high leverage.

Where new bitcoin comes from

New bitcoin is created as a reward to the miners who add each block to the chain. A block is found about every ten minutes, around 144 a day. The miner of each block receives a fixed number of new coins, called the block subsidy, plus the fees paid by the transactions inside it.

The halving

Every 210,000 blocks, about every four years, the subsidy is cut in half. This is written into Bitcoin's rules and happens automatically at a set block height.

Because the reward keeps halving, the total supply approaches 21 million but grows more slowly each period. The last fraction of a bitcoin is expected to be mined around the year 2140.

How much is issued now

At 3.125 BTC per block and about 144 blocks a day, around 450 BTC are created each day.

Over a year that is about 450 × 365 = 164,000 BTC.

With roughly 19.9 million already in circulation, supply grows by about 0.8% a year, and after the next halving by about 0.4%.

At a price of $60,000, the daily issuance is worth about $27 million. That is roughly the amount of new coins miners can sell each day to cover their costs.

What the cap means for market cap maths

Because Bitcoin's supply is known in advance, it is simple to work out the price a given market cap implies.

Price = market cap ÷ circulating supply

A $2 trillion market cap with 19.9 million BTC in circulation is 2,000,000,000,000 ÷ 19,900,000 = about $100,500 per coin.

On the full 21 million supply the same market cap works out to about $95,200.

Lost coins

Some bitcoin will never move again because the keys to it are lost. Estimates vary widely, often between two and four million coins. Those coins still count in the circulating supply that sites report, so the number actually available to trade is lower than the headline figure.

Does the halving raise the price?

Past halvings were followed by large price rises within a year or two, which is why the event gets so much attention. But there have only been four, many other things changed over the same periods, and the halving is known years ahead. Each halving also cuts new supply by a smaller share of the total than the last one did. Treat claims that the halving guarantees a rise with caution.

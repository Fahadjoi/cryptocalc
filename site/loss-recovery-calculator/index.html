Why losses and gains are not the same size

A gain is measured on what you have left, and after a loss you have less. Drop from $10,000 to $5,000 and you have lost 50%. To get back to $10,000 the $5,000 you still hold has to double, which is a 100% gain. The deeper the loss, the faster the climb back gets steeper.

Gain needed = loss ÷ (1 − loss)Both as fractions. A 40% loss: 0.4 ÷ 0.6 = 0.667, so a 66.7% gain.

A worked example

You put in $10,000 and the position is now worth $6,000, a 40% loss.

To get back to $10,000 you need $4,000 of profit on $6,000, which is 4,000 ÷ 6,000 = 66.7%.

At a steady 5% a month, compounding, that takes log(10,000 ÷ 6,000) ÷ log(1.05) = about 10.5 months. At 2% a month it takes more than two years.

The recovery table

Small losses are cheap to undo: 10% needs 11.1%, and 20% needs 25%. Past about 30% the numbers bend sharply upward. A 75% loss needs 300%, and a 90% loss needs 900%. That curve is the reason most trading rules focus on stopping losses early rather than on chasing big wins.

What this means for how you trade

  • Size positions by risk. Risking 1% to 2% per trade keeps a losing streak in the zone where recovery is quick. The position size calculator works this out.
  • Use a stop-loss. A stop turns an open-ended loss into a planned one, before it reaches the steep part of the curve.
  • Watch leverage. At 10× a 5% price move against you is a 50% loss of margin, which then needs 100% to recover.
  • Count fees. Every trade on the way back costs fees, so the real gain needed is slightly higher than the table shows.

Averaging down is not the same as recovering

Buying more at a lower price lowers your average entry, so the coin needs a smaller move to get you to break even. It does not undo the loss on the money already in, and it adds more money to a position that is going against you. The average cost calculator shows what an extra buy would do to your break-even price. Read how much you need to gain to recover from a loss for the longer version.

Common questions

How much do I need to gain to recover a 50% loss?

100%. After a 50% loss you hold half of what you started with, and half has to double to get back to the full amount.

What is the formula for the gain needed after a loss?

Divide the loss by what is left. As fractions: gain = loss ÷ (1 − loss). A 25% loss needs 0.25 ÷ 0.75 = 33.3%.

How long will it take to recover?

It depends on the return you make each month. With compounding, months = log(start ÷ now) ÷ log(1 + monthly return). Few traders make a steady return every month, so treat the estimate as a best case.

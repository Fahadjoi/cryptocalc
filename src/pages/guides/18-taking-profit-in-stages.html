What scaling out means

Instead of one take-profit order for the whole position, you set several, each selling part of it at a higher price. A common plan sells a third at each of two targets and keeps the last third running with the stop moved to the entry price.

A worked example

You buy 0.1 BTC at $60,000, a $6,000 position.

Target 1: sell a third at $66,000 (+10%). Profit on that third: $200.

Target 2: sell a third at $72,000 (+20%). Profit on that third: $400.

The last third stays open with its stop moved up to $60,000. Fees are left out to keep the sums simple.

How it compares in three outcomes

What the price doesSell all at $72,000Scale out as above
Reaches $66,000, then falls back to $60,000$0$200
Reaches $72,000, then falls back to $60,000$1,200$600
Runs on to $84,000$1,200$1,400 ($200 + $400 + $800 on the last third)

Scaling out gives up some profit when the price hits the single target exactly, and in exchange it banks something when the move stalls early and keeps a share of a move that runs further. It does not make a trade more profitable on average. It changes the spread of outcomes.

Fees count for every part

Each partial sale pays its own trading fee. On a percentage fee the total is about the same as one sale of the same size, but some exchanges have a minimum fee per order, which makes many tiny sales expensive. Check the numbers in the profit calculator, and see crypto profit after fees.

Setting the targets

  • Put the first target where it pays for the risk. If the first sale alone covers what the trade risked, the rest of the position is close to a free ride.
  • Move the stop once the first target fills. Raising it to the entry price means the remaining position can no longer lose money, apart from fees and slippage.
  • Decide the split in advance. Thirds and halves are common. What matters is choosing before the trade, not while watching the price.

Scaling in is the same idea in reverse

Buying a position in parts, rather than all at once, spreads the entry price. See DCA vs lump sum and average cost for how the average entry is worked out.

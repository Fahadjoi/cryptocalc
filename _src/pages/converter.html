How the conversion is worked out

Every coin and currency in the list has a live price in US dollars. To convert between any two, the converter turns your amount into dollars and then into the target.

Result = amount × (price of what you have in USD) ÷ (price of what you want in USD)

That is how one feed of dollar prices covers every pair, including coin to coin pairs like ETH to BTC and currency to coin pairs like PKR to USDT. Rates refresh once a minute while the page is open.

A worked example

You hold 0.5 ETH and want to know its value in Bitcoin.

If ETH trades at $2,000 and BTC at $70,000, then 0.5 ETH is worth $1,000.

$1,000 ÷ $70,000 = 0.014286 BTC.

Why your exchange shows a different number

The rate here is a market average across many exchanges. The amount you actually receive when you trade will be a little lower, for four reasons.

  • Trading fees. Usually 0.1% to 0.8% per order. The fee calculator shows what ten exchanges charge.
  • Spread. The price you can sell at is always slightly below the price you can buy at.
  • Local currency premiums. In countries where banks limit crypto transfers, coins often change hands on peer-to-peer markets above or below the official exchange rate. Rupee, naira and lira prices on those markets can differ from the figure here by several percent.
  • Timing. Prices move every second, and this page refreshes every minute.

Stablecoins are close to a dollar, not exactly a dollar

USDT and USDC are designed to hold a value of one US dollar, and most of the time they trade within a tenth of a cent of it. The converter uses their real market price, so 1,000 USDT may show as $999.20 or $1,000.40. On large amounts that difference is real money, which is why it is shown and not rounded away.

Reading very small and very large numbers

Coins priced below one cent need more decimal places than a normal currency, so the converter shows up to eight. Going the other way, one Bitcoin is worth tens of millions of rupees or naira, so large results are grouped with commas. If a result looks wrong by a factor of a thousand, check that you have not mixed up the two boxes, and use the swap button to flip them.

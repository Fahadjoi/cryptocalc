Why the climb back is steeper

A loss is measured against the amount you started with. The recovery is measured against the smaller amount that is left, so the same distance in dollars is a bigger percentage on the way up.

You hold $1,000 and it falls 40% to $600.

To get back to $1,000 you need to make $400 on $600.

400 ÷ 600 = 66.7%, not 40%.

The formula

Gain needed = loss ÷ (1 − loss)Write the loss as a decimal: a 40% loss is 0.40, and 0.40 ÷ 0.60 = 0.667, a 66.7% gain.

Small losses are cheap to recover from. Past about 30% the required gain starts to run away, and a 90% drawdown needs the asset to go up ten times just to get back to even.

Fees make it a little steeper again

If you sell after the fall and buy back in later, each trade costs a fee. At 0.1% a side, getting out and back in costs about 0.2% of the position, so a 50% loss needs roughly a 100.4% gain instead of 100%. The difference is small for one round trip and adds up for traders who sell and rebuy often.

Averaging down changes the break-even, not the loss

Buying more after a fall lowers your average entry, so the price does not need to climb all the way back for the whole position to break even. It also puts more money into the same asset that just fell. See average cost and averaging down for how the new break-even is worked out.

What this means for risk

  • Keep single losses small. A trader who loses 2% on a bad trade needs about 2% to recover. One who loses 30% needs almost 43%.
  • Leverage makes deep drawdowns quick. At 10x, a 5% move against you is a 50% loss of margin. See how leverage works.
  • Size positions on purpose. Risking a fixed small share of the account per trade keeps any one loss in the cheap part of the table. See position sizing.
@faq Q: Why does a 50% loss need a 100% gain? A: After a 50% loss you have half of what you started with. Getting back to the original amount means doubling that half, which is a 100% gain. Q: Is the recovery maths different for crypto than for stocks? A: No. The formula is the same for any asset. Crypto just has larger and more frequent drawdowns, so the steep end of the table comes up more often. Q: Does averaging down help me recover faster?

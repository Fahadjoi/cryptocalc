Isolated vs cross margin

The margin mode is one switch on the order form, and it decides whether a bad trade can lose only its own stake or the whole futures balance.

Isolated margin

With isolated margin, each position has its own pot of money. If the trade is liquidated, that pot is lost and nothing else in the account is touched.

Your futures account holds $5,000. You open a $5,000 long at 10× with $500 of isolated margin.

The position is liquidated after a fall of about 9.5%.

The most you can lose is $500. The other $4,500 stays where it is.

Cross margin

With cross margin, the whole futures balance backs every open position. A losing trade draws on the rest of the account to stay open, so it is liquidated much later. When it does happen, far more is lost.

The same $5,000 account and the same $5,000 long, this time on cross margin.

The full $5,000 supports the position, so in practice it behaves like 1× leverage and a 9.5% fall does not close it.

A 9.5% fall still costs $475, and the loss keeps growing for as long as the price falls and the position stays open.

When each is used

  • Isolated suits single directional trades. You know the worst case before you enter, and one mistake cannot spread.
  • Cross suits hedged positions. If you hold a long and a short that offset each other, cross margin lets the gain on one support the loss on the other.
  • Cross needs a stop-loss more, not less. A far-off liquidation price feels safe, but without a stop a position can lose a large part of the account long before liquidation.

A common mistake

Traders sometimes switch to cross margin because isolated positions keep getting liquidated. That removes the symptom. If trades are being liquidated often, the leverage is too high for the size of the moves, and cross margin only makes the eventual loss larger. Lower leverage and a stop-loss sized with a fixed risk per trade deal with the cause.

Common questions

Which margin mode is safer for beginners?

Isolated, because the loss on any one trade is capped at the margin you gave it.

Can I change margin mode with a position open?

It depends on the exchange. Many require you to close the position first, so check before you open the trade.

Does the calculator on this site use isolated or cross?

It estimates isolated margin, where the liquidation price depends only on the entry price, leverage and maintenance rate.

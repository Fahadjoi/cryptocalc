What the ratio measures

Risk is the distance from your entry to your stop-loss. Reward is the distance from your entry to your target. Divide reward by risk and you have the ratio, often written as 2R or 1:2.

Entry $60,000, stop $58,200, target $63,600.

Risk: $1,800 per coin. Reward: $3,600 per coin.

Ratio: 3,600 ÷ 1,800 = 2. You stand to make twice what you stand to lose.

The win rate that breaks even

Break-even win rate = 1 ÷ (1 + ratio)

A high ratio does not make a trade good on its own. Distant targets are hit less often, so a 5R setup that wins one time in ten still loses money. The ratio and the win rate have to be judged together.

Expectancy: the average result per trade

Expectancy = (win rate × ratio) − loss rateMeasured in units of risk. Multiply by the money you risk per trade to get an amount.

A strategy wins 40% of the time at a ratio of 2.

Expectancy: (0.40 × 2) − 0.60 = +0.2

Risking $100 per trade, the average trade makes about $20, although any single trade is either +$200 or −$100.

A positive expectancy only shows up over many trades. Twenty trades is a small sample, and a run of six or seven losses is ordinary at a 40% win rate.

Fees lower the ratio

Fees make every win smaller and every loss bigger. Take the example above with a position of $3,333 and a 0.1% fee per trade.

Win at the target: $200 minus about $6.87 in fees = $193.13

Loss at the stop: $100 plus about $6.57 in fees = $106.57

Real ratio: 193.13 ÷ 106.57 = 1.81, and the break-even win rate rises from 33.3% to 35.6%

The effect grows as targets get smaller. On a trade aiming for a 0.5% move, the fees can take a third of the reward.

Using it in practice

  • Set the stop where the idea is wrong, not where the ratio looks best. A stop moved closer to improve the ratio gets hit more often.
  • Record your real results. Your own win rate and average win and loss are the only numbers that tell you whether a strategy works.
  • Size every trade the same way. The sums above assume each trade risks the same amount. See position sizing for how.
@faq Q: Is a 1:2 risk-reward ratio good? A: It is a common target because it breaks even at a 33% win rate. Whether it is good depends on how often your setups actually reach the target. Q: Can I be profitable with a ratio below 1? A: Yes, if you win often enough. At a ratio of 0.5 you need to win more than two trades in three, after fees. Q: Does the ratio include fees? A: The simple version does not. For short-term trades, subtract fees from the reward and add them to the risk before you divide. @end

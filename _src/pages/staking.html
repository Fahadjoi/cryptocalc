How staking rewards are worked out

Staking pays a percentage of your balance over time, in the same coin you staked. The calculator steps through the period one day at a time. Each day it adds that day's share of the yearly rate, and on the schedule you choose it moves the collected rewards into the staked balance so they start earning too.

Balance after one year = amount × (1 + rate ÷ periods) ^ periodsPeriods is how many times a year rewards are restaked: 365 for daily, 52 for weekly, 12 for monthly.

A worked example

You stake $1,000 at 5% a year and restake the rewards daily.

After one year: 1,000 × (1 + 0.05 ÷ 365) ^ 365 = $1,051.27.

Without restaking you would have $1,050.00. Compounding added $1.27 in the first year, and the gap widens every year after: over ten years it is $648.66 against $500.00.

APR and APY

APR is the plain yearly rate with no compounding. APY is what you actually earn in a year once rewards are restaked. A 5% APR compounded daily is a 5.13% APY, and a 20% APR compounded daily is a 22.13% APY. Platforms tend to advertise whichever number looks larger, so check which one you are being shown. Enter the APR here and the calculator reports the APY.

The price matters more than the rate

Rewards are paid in the coin, so their value in dollars moves with the coin's price. A 5% reward does not help much if the coin falls 40% while you hold it. The price change field shows that plainly: it applies the change to your whole final balance, the original stake and the rewards together.

High reward rates often belong to small or new coins whose supply is growing quickly. When everyone's balance grows by 15% a year, each coin tends to be worth less, and the real return can be far below the headline rate.

Costs and catches to check first

  • Commission. Validators and exchanges usually keep a share of rewards, often 5% to 25% of them. Enter it in the fee field, or use the net rate your platform quotes and leave the fee at zero.
  • Lock-up and unbonding. Many networks hold your coins for days or weeks after you ask to withdraw, and you cannot sell during that time.
  • Variable rates. Staking rates change as more or fewer people stake. The rate you enter is assumed to hold for the whole period.
  • Slashing. On some networks a validator that misbehaves or goes offline loses part of the stake, and its delegators share the loss.
  • Platform risk. Coins held in an exchange's earn product are a loan to that company. If it fails, they can be lost.
  • Tax. In many countries each reward counts as income when it is received.
@faq Q: How do I calculate staking rewards? A: Multiply your staked amount by the yearly rate and by the fraction of a year you stake. $1,000 at 5% for one year earns $50. If rewards are restaked, each period's rewards also earn rewards, which lifts the one-year total to $51.27 with daily compounding. Q: What is the difference between APR and APY? A: APR is the simple yearly rate. APY includes the effect of compounding. The same product has a higher APY than APR whenever rewards are restaked, for example 5% APR is 5.13% APY with daily compounding. Q: Does compounding frequency make a big difference? A: Not at modest rates. At 5% a year, daily compounding earns $51.27 on $1,000 and monthly compounding earns $51.16. The gap grows at high rates and over many years. Q: Are staking rewards guaranteed? A: No. Rates vary with network conditions, validators can be penalised, and the coin's price can fall by more than the rewards you earn. Q: Why does the result change when I enter a price change? A: Rewards and your original stake are both held in the coin. If the coin's price is different at the end, the dollar value of everything you hold changes by the same percentage. @end
